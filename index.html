From science fiction,
to reality for people with neurological disease

Over a century of development. First only heard of in science fiction books, then seen in science fiction movies, to now extraordinary real life breakthroughs with an extremely exciting future.

Background video: Michigan Medicine (2025).

Abstract

The first ever electroencephalogram (EEG) (brain electrical activity) was recorded in 1924 by Hans Berger. A brain-computer interface reads electrical activity in the brain and turns it into a command for a computer. This project explores what people were able to achieve in the early stages, what people are able to achieve now, what developers claim they will be able to achieve in the future and what actually looks possible in the future according to existing evidence. After 100 years of development, patients with neurological disease have been recorded to have controlled robotic limbs with their brain, but this technology introduces major health considerations like the risk of having invasive brain surgery. This project aims to investigate science-fiction-like claims made by accredited people to figure out if these claims hold any real value and have an actual chance of helping patients with conditions like severe paralysis or locked-in syndrome.
